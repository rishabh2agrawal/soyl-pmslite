1.2 About SOYL AI
SOYL AI is building an AI-native Property Management System for independent Indian
hotels. Our hypothesis is that the next generation of PMS is not a database with a UI on top
ΓÇö it is a multi-agent system where guests and staff can interact with the property in natural
language, across voice and text, in their own language.
Our core product, SOYL Core, sells at Γé╣45,000 per month per property. We are live in one
30-room hotel, with a pipeline of fifteen to twenty-five properties in active evaluation. Our
stack is FastAPI plus LangGraph plus pgvector plus Supabase plus Redis on the backend,
with an Asterisk PBX and Twilio SIP voice layer that runs at sub-three-second end-to-end
latency. We fine-tune LLaMA 3.1 8B with QLoRA on hospitality data, and we are exploring
AMD Ryzen AI NPU edge inference.
We have a sister company, SOYL Agency, which builds digital products as a services arm
and shares roughly seventy percent of the engineering stack.
Your project, SOYL Lite, is a new product tier that we have not built yet. It targets a different
and much larger market: the small independent hotels, homestays, and guesthouses below
Γé╣2,000 average daily rate, which currently run on paper. The reasons we are building Lite,
and why it cannot be a stripped-down version of Core, are explained in Part 2 of this
document.

Part 2 ΓÇö Context: Lessons from the First Pilot
2.1 What happened
In early 2026, SOYL AI deployed its first production pilot at a thirty-room independent hotel
in India. The property was newly under new ownership, had inherited a pen-and-paper
operation, and had a Google Maps rating between 3.4 and 3.8 stars. Average daily rate was
between rupees 1,500 and 1,800.
Our hypothesis was simple. A modern, AI-assisted PMS designed to reduce clicks, surface
the right information at the right time, and minimize training overhead would be
unambiguously easier than a paper register. Adoption would follow.
Within fifteen days, the manager told us the software was hard to use. For a product whose
core differentiator is usability, that feedback was sobering. We took it seriously and went
deep.
What we found was not a UI problem.
The manager was not struggling with our software. He was unmotivated to use any
software. "Hard to use" was, in plain terms, a polite way of saying "I don't want to."
At thirty rooms, with one manager who has spent years internalizing a paper system, a
paper register really is faster for him. No login. No screen. No keyboard. He knows where
every number lives because he wrote it himself five minutes ago. The cognitive cost of
switching to any digital system, no matter how well-designed, is real ΓÇö and at thirty rooms,
the marginal daily benefit is hard for the manager to feel.
This was the first surprise. The second was bigger.
2.2 The three insights that reshape Lite
Insight 1 ΓÇö The buyer and the user have opposite incentives.
In the small-hotel segment, the owner is the buyer. The manager is the user. Their incentives
diverge.
The owner gains real-time visibility, structured guest communication, audit trails, OTA
reconciliation, and the kind of data hygiene that prevents small failures from compounding
into bad reviews. The owner has every reason to want this product.
The manager gains a daily extra step. A login. A second place to write things down. A way
for the owner to see what is happening when the manager would prefer the owner not see.
The manager has every reason not to want this product.
Most PMS products are designed for the manager because the manager is the person
clicking the buttons. This is correct for Western three-star and above. It is wrong for our
segment. We need to design for the owner, and engineer the manager experience to remove
every excuse not to use it.
Insight 2 ΓÇö Many small Indian hotels prefer transactions stay off the books.

This is delicate, and we will not pretend it does not exist. India's GST regime treats hotel
rooms differently based on tariff. Hotels in the rupees 1,500 to 1,800 ARR band sit close to
thresholds that determine whether and at what rate GST applies. Combined with income tax
considerations, the incentive to under-report occupancy or shift transactions to cash is real,
persistent, and economically rational from the operator's perspective.
A digital PMS, by its nature, creates a record. A complete one. Every check-in, every
walk-in, every cash payment, every extension. For a property that wants to maintain
optionality on what gets reported, a comprehensive digital record is not a feature ΓÇö it is a
liability.
Our position is firm: we will not build features that enable under-reporting. We will not hide
bookings. We will not have a delete-from-records button. SOYL Lite produces complete,
accurate operational records.
Our position is also pragmatic: we will not force every interaction into a tax-document
workflow. Generating a GST invoice is an explicit owner action, not an automatic
consequence of a booking. A walk-in cash transaction can be recorded as a walk-in cash
transaction without that automatically producing a tax invoice with a sequential number. The
operational record is complete; the reporting posture is the owner's call.
If this distinction confuses you, ask Ryan. It matters.
Insight 3 ΓÇö The rating problem is an information problem, not a service
problem.
When we read the negative reviews at the pilot property, the patterns were striking and
consistent. Bookings not honored or assigned to the wrong room category. Guests waiting
on requests ΓÇö extra towels, late check-out, food orders ΓÇö that never reached the right
person. Confusion between front desk and housekeeping over room status. Payment
disputes traced to incomplete or contradictory records.
Almost none of these were service failures. The staff was not rude. The rooms were not dirty.
The food was not bad.
These were information failures. A guest told the front desk something. The front desk
could not reliably get that information to housekeeping, the manager, or the kitchen. By
the time the guest noticed, it was a one-star review.
This is the wedge. SOYL Lite's most valuable feature is not the booking screen. It is the
request tracker ΓÇö every guest request captured, routed, acknowledged, and visible to the
owner. If we can make information stop falling on the floor, ratings recover. If ratings recover,
bookings recover. That is a value paper cannot deliver. That is what the owner is buying.

Three pillars sit underneath every design decision in the PRD that follows. If a design choice
does not serve one of these pillars, it is the wrong choice.
Pillar 1 ΓÇö Owner-first, manager-light.

The owner gets the rich product: real-time dashboards, request tracking, daily WhatsApp
summaries, the things that justify rupees 25,000 per month. The manager gets a
stripped-down interface that is so fast and so simple it beats paper on speed, with no

Pillar 2 ΓÇö Faster than paper on the actions that happen ten times a day.
Manager-mode actions ΓÇö new booking, check-in, check-out, log a request ΓÇö must each
take fewer seconds than the paper equivalent. We measure this. If voice input gets us there,
we use voice. If a single-tap ingestion gets us there, we build it. Easier to learn is not enough
ΓÇö the manager has already learned paper. We need faster to use today, every single day.
Pillar 3 ΓÇö Information never falls on the floor.
Every guest request, every payment, every shift handover produces a record with a status
and an owner. Nothing is held only in someone's head. The owner can see, at any moment,
what is open and who is responsible. This is the feature paper cannot match. This is the
product.

Part 3 ΓÇö Product Requirements Document
3.1 Product vision
What Khatabook did for kirana stores, SOYL Lite does for small hotels ΓÇö but with the
owner, not the staff, as the primary beneficiary.
SOYL Lite is the entry-tier (rupees 25,000 per month) product in the SOYL family. It targets
the largest underserved segment of Indian hospitality: small, independent,
sub-rupees-2,000-ARR hotels, homestays, and guesthouses still operating on pen, paper,
and WhatsApp.
SOYL Lite is not a stripped-down version of SOYL Core. It is a different product with a
different design language, a different user model, and a different wedge. Core sells AI
agents that handle guest communication; Lite sells operational discipline and owner visibility.
Lite is the gateway product ΓÇö successful Lite hotels graduate to Core as they grow.
3.2 Target users
Primary buyer ΓÇö the absentee or semi-absentee owner
Picture a forty-five to sixty year old hotel owner. Owns a fourteen-to-twenty-five room
property in a tier-two or tier-three city. May own a small business in addition to the hotel.
Visits the property two or three times a week. Is not at the front desk. Trusts the manager but
does not blindly trust the manager. Has been burned by missed bookings, unrecorded cash,
and bad reviews that surprised him. Pays for the software.
This is the person we are designing the home screen for. The Owner Mode dashboard is
what convinces him to keep paying rupees 25,000 a month. Every screen in Owner Mode
should answer one of three questions: What is happening right now? What almost went
wrong today? How is the property doing this week?
Primary user ΓÇö the manager / front-desk operator
Picture a thirty-to-fifty year old manager. Has worked at the property for two-to-eight years.
Comfortable with WhatsApp, may or may not be fluent in English, comfortable in Hindi and
the regional language. Has internalized a paper-based system that works for him. Sees
software adoption as risk and effort with no obvious benefit. Will use the product only if it is
meaningfully faster than what he does today, and only if the owner insists.
Manager Mode must respect his constraints. No login screens between him and the booking
he needs to make right now. Voice input for guest names ΓÇö typing Devanagari or Kannada
is slower than speaking. Defaults that work ninety percent of the time. A bottom navigation
he can hit with one thumb while holding a phone in one hand and a key in the other.
Secondary user ΓÇö the modernizer
The owner's daughter, son, or nephew. Twenty to thirty years old. Comfortable with apps.
Will be the one who actually onboards the product, sets it up, trains the manager, and

ΓÇó F&B, restaurant, spa, or POS modules.
ΓÇó Complex BI dashboards ΓÇö RevPAR, ADR trend analysis, etc. These are Pro features.
ΓÇó Multi-user roles and granular permissions. Lite has three fixed roles: owner, manager,

3.4 Design principles
These are not aspirations. They are constraints. Memorize them.
1. Three taps to anything. No screen is more than three taps from the home screen. If it
takes more, redesign.
2. Big targets. Minimum forty-eight by forty-eight pixel touch targets. Owners often have
older eyes and use phones in bright sun.
3. Plain language. No jargon in UI copy. PMS, ARR, RevPAR, reservation ID are all
banned. Use booking number, today's earnings.
4. Speak the user's language, literally. Every string lives in an i18n key. English is the
fallback, never the default.
5. Offline is the rule, not the exception. Assume internet drops mid-checkin. The app
never loses data.
6. One-thumb operation. Bottom nav, sticky action buttons at thumb-reach, never make
the user reach the top corner.
7. Manager Mode beats paper on speed. Every common action is measured against the
time it takes to do it on paper. Slower than paper means the design is wrong.
8. Owner Mode shows what almost went wrong. The dashboard surfaces issues, not just
metrics. Three requests still open beats average response time 4.2 hours.
9. No empty states without a CTA. If today has no arrivals, say so and offer the next
useful action.

A. Glossary
Terms you will hear in standups, PRs, and customer calls.
Term Meaning
ADR Average Daily Rate. Total room revenue divided by rooms sold.
ARR Average Room Rate. Used interchangeably with ADR in Indian
hospitality.
RevPAR Revenue Per Available Room. ADR times occupancy. Pro-tier metric,
not in Lite.
OTA Online Travel Agency. Booking.com, MakeMyTrip, Agoda, Goibibo.
PMS Property Management System. The category we are in.
FRRO Foreigners Regional Registration Office. Mandatory foreign-guest
reporting.
C-Form Foreign guest declaration form, filed with FRRO.
GST Goods and Services Tax. Indian indirect tax.
DPDP Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. India's data privacy law.
RLS Row-Level Security. Supabase/PostgreSQL feature for tenant isolation.
PWA Progressive Web App. Installable web app, no app store.
RSC React Server Component. Next.js 14 feature.
SAC Service Accounting Code. SAC 996311 covers room rentals.
HSN Harmonized System Nomenclature. Goods classification code.

What I'm trying to do: one sentence.
What I tried: bullet list.
What is happening: error message or unexpected behavior.
What I think the problem is: your hypothesis.

no shipping. Asking after thirty minutes of effort is professional, not weak.
